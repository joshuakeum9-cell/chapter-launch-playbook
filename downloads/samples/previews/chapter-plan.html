Climate Tech Austin

Launch plan

Thursday, October 1, 2026 to Wednesday, October 14, 2026

Bulleted lines are the steps the toolkit and the Climate Tech Cities team depend on. The grey notes are advice: take what is useful and skip the rest. You know your city better than this document does.

The full manual is the chapter launch playbook: https://joshuakeum9-cell.github.io/chapter-launch-playbook/. This plan is the short version with your dates in it.

Before you start

•     Create a project in Claude and name it Climate Tech Austin.

•     Add Chapter_Settings_Austin.docx to that project as a context file, in project settings under add files, not attached to a chat. Every skill reads it from there, so nothing else in the toolkit works until it is in.

•     Install the toolkit: ctc-chapter-setup, ctc-newsletter, ctc-partner-map.

•     Install the Claude in Chrome extension. The weekly harvest needs it to read Luma and Eventbrite.

•     Turn on code execution in Settings, Capabilities.

 

The playbook's Install page has these in order with links. Skills only appear in a fresh conversation, so start a new one after installing.

Week 1

October 1 to October 7

Logo

•     Open the Logo step on the chapter launch playbook. It has the prompt with your city in it, the reference images to attach, and the full walkthrough.

•     Generate concepts in ChatGPT and pick one.

•     Send your pick to the Climate Tech Cities team for sign-off. Nothing with the logo on it goes public until it is approved: it ends up on the Substack, the website, and every event graphic, so changing it later means changing it everywhere.

 

Judge concepts at icon size. That is the size most people will ever see it at.

Substack

•     Once the logo is approved, send it and your chapter name (Climate Tech Austin) to the Climate Tech Cities team. They create the Substack publication and send you the login.

•     Confirm the web address with them at the same time. Your settings file records austin.climatetechcities.com, but there is no fixed rule yet, and once other chapters link to the address it cannot change quietly.

•     When you have the login, ask Claude in your project: "walk me through Substack setup" and "draft my welcome email". Both read your settings file.

 

Two settings worth doing early: the sender name as Test Name rather than the chapter, because email from a person gets opened more often, and the welcome email, because Substack sends it to every new subscriber automatically and it is otherwise blank.

Week 2

October 8 to October 14

Source list

•     Run ctc-source-map in your project. It asks whether you have calendar links of your own, researches the rest, and hands back your source list to review.

•     When it looks right, add CTC_Source_Registry_Austin.xlsx to your project as a context file. The weekly harvest reads it and stops without it.

 

Every future issue is built from this list, so it is worth an hour now. Sources go quiet without telling anyone, which is why the schedule has a refresh every three months.

Subscriber list

•     Add your first subscribers in Substack and start with whoever you have. A first issue going to a few dozen people is normal.

 

Your own network, asked one by one, is usually most of the early list and converts better than a public post. If you are thinking of importing a list from somewhere, ask Claude "how do I build my list" first: there are consent rules, and stricter ones for chapters in the UK and EU.

First issue

Tuesday, October 13, 2026

•     Say "run the newsletter" in your project. The toolkit runs the harvest, hands you the cut, drafts the issue, and stops wherever your input is needed.

 

A first issue is small and mostly a note about why the newsletter exists. That is the right shape, not a failure. Do not wait until there are more events.

Every week

The issue goes out every Tuesday. Run the four steps in one sitting or spread them across the week.

Step

What happens

Harvest

ctc-harvest sweeps your sources and the reader submission form into a spreadsheet.

Cut

You delete rows and flag your picks.

Draft

ctc-assemble builds the issue. You rewrite the opening note and picks in your own words.

Send

Check the issue, paste into Substack, send.

 

Say "run the newsletter" and the toolkit sequences these and stops where your input is needed.

Every month

•     In the first week of the month, run ctc-opportunities.

•     Add Opportunities_Austin.xlsx to your project as a context file, replacing last month's copy. ctc-assemble reads it every week, so the opportunities block has something in it in every issue.

Every quarter

•     Re-run ctc-source-map once every three months and replace CTC_Source_Registry_Austin.xlsx in your project. Sources go quiet without telling anyone, and the refresh catches a dead calendar before it thins out an issue.

Once the newsletter is running

•     Run ctc-city-resources and name your city. It researches the ecosystem and builds your resource workbook.

•     Then ctc-partner-list turns that workbook into a tiered list of who to approach and what to ask each one for.

•     Then ctc-partner-outreach drafts the messages in your own voice.

 

Partners are optional and have no deadline. Starting them before the newsletter is running is how leads end up finishing neither.

If you miss a week

•     Send the next issue on your usual send day. No apology or explanation needed. The target is weeks covered over a quarter, not consecutive weeks.
