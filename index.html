Week one · in ChatGPT

Your chapter logo

This is the one step that does not run in Claude. The logo is made in ChatGPT, because it generates the images. Everything else on this page happens in your Claude project.

Every chapter logo is the same lockup: a square icon on the left, and Climate Tech [your city] stacked on three lines to the right. Only the building silhouette changes from city to city. That consistency is what makes ten chapters look like one organisation.

Same lavender field, same orange horizon, same two greens, same wordmark, same proportions. Chicago gets the Willis Tower, London gets the clock tower, New York gets the Empire State. Only the silhouette moves. That is the whole system, and matching it is the entire job.

The icon on its own

The square is where all the work happens. These are the same three chapters with the wordmark cropped away, which is the clearest way to see how far the abstraction goes.

Notice how little detail survives. Two planes, a light side and a dark side, and a handful of steps. If your city's landmark still has windows or a spire made of several pieces, it is not abstracted far enough yet.

Chapter setup · once

      

    


    
Nine questions

    
One at a time, plain language, each with a default you can accept. Reply "default" to any of them and move on. The last one is optional.

    

      
    
        
  1. Which city is your chapter in?Becomes your chapter name, Climate Tech [City], everywhere it appears.
    2. 
        
  2. What is your name?Goes on every issue as the sender, because email from a person gets opened more often than email from an organization.
    3. 
        
  3. Which day of the week do you want to send?Pick one and never move it.
    4. 
        
  4. How often will you send?Weekly is the standard for every chapter, and the default. If your city turns out to have too few events, the weekly cycle offers every two weeks after your first harvests; you do not decide that on day one.
    5. 
        
  5. Which events count as local for your chapter?The answer that does the most work: every week a skill decides whether an event belongs in your issue, and this sentence is the only instruction it gets.
    6. 
        
  6. What web address should the newsletter use?Chapters use two patterns and there is no published rule. It records your preference and flags it to confirm with the Climate Tech Cities team.
    7. 
        
  7. How do you want to close each issue?Default: [your first name] and the Climate Tech [City] team.
    8. 
        
  8. What timezone are you in?It offers the obvious one and you confirm.
    9. 
        
  9. Anything unusual about your city?A second cluster somewhere, a university that dominates the calendar, a season when everything stops. Skippable: say no and it goes straight to building.

Week two

    
Build your source list

    
Everywhere events get announced in your city. Every harvest only finds what this list points at.


    

      
Newsletter · start here

      

    

    
With no source list yet, Claude starts here.


    

      
Claude asks first whether you have your own links

      
You probably already check five or ten calendars by hand. Those are the best rows in the registry, because a real person has already vouched for them. Claude lays out three ways to go and waits for your answer.

      
    
        
  1. Paste your links, then Claude researches tooIt verifies yours, follows their cross-links, and works the full nine-category search on top. Best coverage, and the recommended option for a new chapter.
    2. 
        
  2. Paste your links onlyIt verifies what you give it and stops. Fastest, and a fair choice if you already know your city's scene and just want the registry file built.
    3. 
        
  3. Claude researches from scratchNo links needed. It goes and finds them, then brings back a verified list for you to review.
    4. 
      

      
Paste links straight into your first message and Claude takes that as your answer rather than making you pick from a menu.

    


    
Claude searches Luma, Eventbrite, universities, incubators, city government, professional groups and arts venues. Every source it finds, and every link you paste, gets five checks:

    
    
      
  • It loads
    • 
      
  • It ran something in the last 90 days
    • 
      
  • It repeats on a pattern
    • 
      
  • It is a real, climate-relevant organization
    • 
      
  • Its web address is stable
    • 
    

    
If a source fails a check, Claude names the check instead of dropping it quietly, so you decide.

    
Read the list before you approve it. You live there and Claude does not, so add the meetup everyone goes to if it is missing.

    
The target for a new chapter is 25 to 30 sources. Claude works all nine categories and follows every cross-link before it stops; if that comes to fewer than 25, it hands you the list anyway and tells you which categories came up empty. It never pads. The finished registry comes back as a spreadsheet named CTC_Source_Registry_[City].xlsx.

    
Once you have approved the list, add CTC_Source_Registry_[City].xlsx to your project as a context file. Same place as your settings file: project settings, then add files. The weekly harvest reads it from there.

    
One row is the same in every chapter's list: the Climate Tech Cities event submission form. Readers of your newsletter send you events through it, and the harvest reads your city's submissions every week alongside your calendars.

    
Re-run this once every three months. Calendars die quietly, and the quarterly refresh is what catches a dead one before your issues go thin. Nothing else in the toolkit runs quarterly.

Step one

    
Collect Claude

    
Paste the prompt into your project. Claude sweeps every source in your registry and hands back a spreadsheet. It collects everything without filtering, on purpose: collecting and judging are separate jobs, and mixing them turns a short task into a long one.

    

      
Harvest · weekly

      

    

    
It also reads the Climate Tech Cities event submission form and pulls in anything a reader sent in for your city, marked submitted in a Source column so you can see where each row came from. Every submitted link gets opened and checked like any other.

    
Before it sweeps, Claude asks whether you already know about anything that will not be on a calendar. Paste it in and it goes into the sheet as lead. Every row says where it came from: registry, submitted, or lead.

    
The spreadsheet marks anything incomplete: the empty cell is shaded red and named in a Missing Info column, so you can see at a glance what needs a second look.

    
If Claude asks to use your Chrome browser, say yes. Some event sites hide their listings from simple requests, so Claude reads those pages in your browser instead. It is reading, not clicking. Save the spreadsheet when it finishes.

Step two

    
Cut You

    
Open the spreadsheet and delete rows. There is no prompt for this one, because it is judgment. How many events end up in the newsletter is decided here, and there is no target number: a good week is however many good events your city actually has.

    

      

        
Delete

        
    
          
  • Anything outside your scope rule, which is written in your settings file for exactly this moment
    • 
          
  • Anything not really climate
    • 
          
  • Anything with a dead link or no date
    • 
          
  • Duplicates. If the same event came from two calendars, keep the one with the better registration link
    • 
          
  • Rows marked submitted were sent in by a reader. Treat them like any other row; if you cut one, it is worth a reply to whoever sent it
    • 
        

      

      

        
Flag

        
    
          
  • Note four to six events worth featuring, roughly one per day that has events
    • 
          
  • Type PICK in a spare column next to each one
    • 
          
  • Favour events that are free, easy for a newcomer, in person, or hard to find elsewhere
    • 
          
  • Claude reads the PICK column at the next step, so the picks question is a one-line confirmation
    • 
        

      

    

    
Upload the edited spreadsheet back when you are done. The issue gets built from that file, not the one Claude handed you, so check you are attaching the version you just edited.

Troubleshooting

    
When something breaks

    

      
Harvest returned almost nothing
A source broke; sites change. Ask Claude which sources returned zero, then check those by hand. Do not assume the city went quiet.

      
A calendar stopped working
Mark it manual in your source registry and check it yourself each week.

      
Claude keeps asking to use my browser
Normal, and expected on Luma, Eventbrite, and the submission form. Say yes. It is reading pages, not clicking anything.

      
A page is asking me to prove I am human
Claude will stop and tell you. Open the page yourself and paste what you see. Claude will not solve these.

      
The finished issue sounds like a robot
The yellow rewrite lines were deleted without rewriting the blocks above them. Those three blocks are the only part readers remember; put them in your own words.

      
A reader emailed me an event
Send them the submission form link at the bottom of any issue. Anything submitted for your city shows up in the next harvest, marked submitted.

      
Assembly stopped and said the fixed links are not ready
The plugin refuses to build an issue with a placeholder in the platforms block or the Join the fun block. Remove those two blocks from the JSON and the standard text is inserted with the real links; you never type them.

      
Formatting broke when I pasted into Substack
Almost always the Word document, or a copy button. Do it this way instead:
        
    
          
  1. Open the HTML file in your browser. Not the Word document
    2. 
          
  2. Click just before the first word at the very top of the issue
    3. 
          
  3. Hold the mouse button down and drag all the way to the bottom, past the last line, so the whole issue is highlighted
    4. 
          
  4. Copy the selection. Ctrl+C on Windows, Cmd+C on a Mac
    5. 
          
  5. Open a new Substack post and paste. Ctrl+V or Cmd+V
    6. 
        

        Dragging to select is the part that matters. It copies the formatting and the links along with the text, which a copy button does not.

      
Claude does not know my chapter settings
Check the settings file is on the project, not a chat, then start a fresh conversation.

      
I do not have a paid Claude account
The skills need one. Every step can also be done by hand: the source list, the weekly sweep, the cut, and the write-up are ordinary work, and chapters did them manually before the toolkit existed. It takes longer, not worse.

      
I missed a week
Send the next one. No apology and no explanation. The target is weeks covered over a quarter.

      
My chapter has a co lead
Everything works the same. Decide who owns the send.
